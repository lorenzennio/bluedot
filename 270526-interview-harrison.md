**3. What I’ve Done Since**

- Started working in the German Parliament.
- Learned quickly that evidence rarely speaks for itself.
- In policy, assumptions often determine the conclusion.
- Scientific training is useful because you can ask: which assumptions are defensible, and which ones are doing the real work?
- Politics requires balancing truth, feasibility, legitimacy, and public trust.
- This made me more interested in evidence-informed governance, especially for high-uncertainty technologies.

> As a scientist, you ask what is true. In politics, you also have to ask what is implementable, legitimate, and robust under public pressure.


**4. Concrete Example: Nuclear Energy**

- Technically and economically, one can make a cautious argument for nuclear energy.
- But many studies simplify the political and social initial conditions.
- Public trust, timelines, party politics, and institutional capacity matter.
- Lesson: policy analysis must include the system it is trying to intervene in.
- This is where scientific thinking helps.


**5. Covid Enquete**

- Work focused on evaluating past measures and preparing future recommendations.
- Highly complex system, emotionally charged topic.
- Scientific mindset is especially valuable when evidence, values, and public trust collide.
- Big bottleneck: comparison. Without better data, we cannot know which interventions worked best.
- Need better evaluation infrastructure, regulatory sandboxes, and policy experimentation.
- Best recommendations are not only correct; they are simple, feasible, cheap enough, and politically legible.

> The bottleneck is comparison: without good data, every side can tell a plausible story.


**6. Advice / Meta-Point**

- We need to treat each other more as collaborators.
- Do not only ask: "What do I want government to do?"
- Ask: “What problem is government already trying to solve, and how does my proposal help?”
- For AI safety: safe AI for resilient critical infrastructure is often a stronger argument than hypothetical doomsday scenarios.

**What BlueDot Course you took, why and how did it change things?**
- AGI strategy, while starting new role in German Gov.
- Really nice discussion group: very different people with very different backgrounds.
  - Identifying a common ground for a discussion.
  - What are our respective values, or which currency do we trade in?

- Taught me the value of collaboration and shared responsibilities: science, politics, industry need to collaborate. 
  - Science evaluates
  - Politics implements
  - Industry scales

- Made me think ito capital and common responsibility
  - **Democratic politics seeks legitimacy** (voter trust, regulatory capacity)
  - **Academia seeks credibility** (high impact journals, citations, collaboration)
  - **Industry seeks profitability** (returns, growth, market share)
- Interactions are interesting: how do we exchange currencies?
- Politics:
  - Everyone tends to think "What do I want from the government?"
  - But: governments job is to prioritize (economy or education or infrastructure or welfare)
    - Democratic governments priorities ideally reflect voters concerns.

- You want the **government to act on your concerns**? You have 2 options
  - Convince the voters that your problem is important or
  - Start offering the governments' currency.
- Advice to technical people: don’t just ask “what should government do?” Ask “what problem is government already trying to solve, and how does my proposal help them solve it?”

- As an example: "Regulate AI to make it safe" vs. "Safe AI is necessary for making resilient critical infrastructure more effective."


**What great stuff have you gone on to do since.**

- Started working in the German Parliament.
- As a scientist the first thing that shocked me: For every opinion there is a study supporting it.
  - Evidence does not speak for itself; assumptions matter.
  - The reason is simply complexity: there are so many degrees of freedom which can be tuned and assumptions made, to make the outcome look as desired
  - This is why scientists are especially useful: Which assumptions are defensible, which are load-bearing, and which change the conclusion?
  - Example: Should Germany restart using nuclear energy?
    - From a purely economic standpoint - careful yes from my side. 
    - Studies ignore socio-economic aspects and political consequences - their assumptions on the initial state do not hold.
- This is what makes politics hard: you need to critically evaluate and balance so many different opinions.

 
**Covid Enquete**
- Goal: evaluation of measures and future recommendations
- Highly complex system + very emotional = scientific mindset important
- Best recommendations? Ones which deliver the largest political capital. Ideally, align with political agenda (+ simple, cheap, effective).
  - Hot tip for convincing politicians: Identify how your solution aligns with their values and agenda. Connect your proposal to legitimacy, feasibility, and the government’s existing priorities.
- The bottleneck is comparison: without good data, we cannot know which interventions worked.
  - *There is only one god in science: experiment.*
  - Experimental governance: regulatory sandboxes, pilots, better administrative data, pre-registered policy evaluations.
